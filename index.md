Growing up in South Florida, the aquatic environment was all around me. I frequently spent my days at the beach, snorkeling in the water or collecting shells on the beach. Having ready access to the ocean sparked a love and appreciation for the aquatic environment. 

In the fall of 2019, I began at the University of Florida's Honors Program, where I graduated summa cum laude with a B.S. in Marine Sciences and a B.S. in Environmental Science. I also completed minors in Economics and Zoology, as well as the CALS Honors Scholar certificate. As a freshman, I joined the [Executive Board of Science Olympiad at the University of Florida](https://uf.floridascienceolympiad.org/), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit dedicated to providing K-12 STEM Education through team-based competitions. I was President in March 2020, a role I served in for two and a half years. I continue to serve as an emeritus member and assist with logistics for tournaments at UF and other universities around the country. I worked in the [Siders Lab](https://www.zachsiders.com/), examining time-at-depth weighted biodiversity trends for sharks of the North Pacific.  You can read more about this project on the [Research](willpatrone.github.io/research) page. 

After graduating in May 2022, I made the cross-country move to Seattle, Washington, where I accepted a job as a research coordinator with UW's Department of Chemistry. I worked with 45 faculty members on grant acquisitions and renewals, providing support on budgets, sponsor-specific requirements, and university bureaucracy. In January 2023, I transitioned to the Department of Urology and Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center to work as a research coordinator with [Canary PASS](https://canarypass.org/). PASS is an active surveillance study of prostate cancer for men across the US and Canada. I worked on specimen management, data acquisition, cleaning, and organization, and served as a pseudo-project manager for specimen data use within research studies. 

In September 2023, I joined the [PuntLab](https://puntlab.washington.edu/) as a master's student under Dr. Andre Punt. My thesis explores multispecies fisheries management of the US West Coast Groundfish fishery, defining métiers to calculate multispecies target reference points. Outside of my thesis work, I am working with Dr. Ray Hilborn on a global analysis of trends in marine biodiversity on the stock assessment for Widow Rockfish, and updating the Atlantis ecosystem model for the Gulf of Alaska. You can read more about my current work on the [Research](willpatrone.github.io/research) page.
