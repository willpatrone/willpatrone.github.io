## Multispecies Management of West Coast Groundfish
No species lives in isolation; for my master's thesis, I am examining possible multispecies management strategies for the US West Coast groundfish fishery. A métier analysiswas cunducted using landing records from 2011-2023, which were run through a Clustering LARge Applications (CLARA) clustering algorithm. These combinations of port, gear, landing month, and species composition were divided into 17 clusters. The results from the CLARA clustering were then refined into four métiers, each with unique gear and species compositions. My second chapter applies these métiers to multispecies target reference points, particularly maximum sustainable yield (MSY) and maximum economic yield (MEY). Effort and economic data was gathered from vessels in the same time period, as well as biological parameters for each species, and these target reference points were estimated for each of the four métiers.

## Stock Assessment
Through a collaboration between UW and NOAA's NWFSC, graduate students can work under the guidance of stock assessment experts to produce a stock assessment for a groundfish species on the US West coast. I was part of the 2025 update assessment for the Widow rockfish (*Sebastes entomelas*). My role included data cleaning, processing, and modelling in R (including using the packages *r4ss* and *nwfscDiag*), running likelihood estimates for biological parameters, updating species information with new findings, and writing and editing the final stock assessment document.
